## Evidence and uncertainty

- [FRUS1977d168: CIA, Rhodesia: The Parties to the Dispute](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v16/d168) (1977-09-16). Reports ZIPRA cadres receiving both guerrilla and conventional training, including overseas training, while rapid expansion also includes trainees. Reports ZANLA training and discipline weaknesses. This is a contemporary intelligence judgement, not an impartial census or a rating for every fighter. Access: Full primary text read.

- [FRUS1965d432: Department of State, Projected Course of the Mozambique Rebellion](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v24/d432) (1965-05-10). Distinguishes trained officer-potential cadres from recruits receiving short training; highlights shortages of trained leadership and offensive capability. Contains Portuguese claims that should not be treated as objective descriptions of every FRELIMO fighter. Access: Full primary text read.

- [CIA1973Mozambique: CIA Mozambique national intelligence survey retrospective](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP01-00707R000200100012-2.pdf) (1973). Describes initial overseas training, subsequent cadre training and small-unit operations; supports development beyond the initial 1965 snapshot, but does not establish an exact month when every unit improved. Access: Official indexed primary text; direct PDF redirected to CIA homepage in this run.

- [CIA1985AfricaReview: CIA Africa Review ALA AR85-022](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP87T00289R000100430001-1.pdf) (1985-10-04). Describes ill-trained regular forces, desertion and draft avoidance, equipment-operation difficulties, and dependence on Zimbabwean assistance. Supports a modest late-war readiness reduction rather than a universal extreme rating. Access: Official indexed primary passage; direct PDF redirected to CIA homepage in this run.

- [CIA1985Mozambique: CIA Mozambique assessment, RDP85T01058R000100440004-4](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP85T01058R000100440004-4.pdf) (1985). Describes leadership, morale and supply problems, and gradual prospective benefits of foreign retraining. Corroborates the 1985 readiness judgement; precise publication day not established here. Access: Official indexed primary passage only.

- [UNONUMOZ: UN ONUMOZ background: formation of Mozambican defence force](https://peacekeeping.un.org/sites/default/files/past/onumozFT.htm) (1993-1994). Instructor training in 1993 and infantry training in 1994 accompanied the new army formation. Integration was an ongoing process, not instant improvement in tactical competence. Access: Official UN narrative read.

- [UN1994report: Secretary-General ONUMOZ progress report S/1994/1002](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n94/343/66/pdf/n9434366.pdf) (1994-08-26). Records incomplete recruitment/training and transfer from the former army in August1994. Supports keeping the new FADM at a restrained baseline rather than automatically raising quality because of the new identity. Access: Official indexed primary passage read.

The1985January break is a bounded calendar-year allocation for the condition documented during1985, not first-source dating or an actual abrupt military event. Mozambique1965–74is deliberately a coarse average: an early1965recruit weakness report cannot establish all fighters across the whole liberation war. Existing identity/service dates are unchanged.

Primary intelligence assessments may contain foreign-policy and adversarial biases. UN1994progress reporting is stronger for integration chronology than for predicting tactical performance. No extreme morale penalty derives from political defeat, ethnicity or equipment origin.
